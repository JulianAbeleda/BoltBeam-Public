# Replay workload: the 27B hybrid on the RTX 5090, 2026-10-11

A replay plays a recorded session through the user's own llama-server command and shows, per turn and for the
session, where the time goes against the roofline. It shows where. It does not say why.

## What was built

- `boltbeam.replay_trace.v1` (`boltbeam/trace/replay_trace.py`). A session as counts per turn: new tokens, tool
  tokens, reply tokens, a compaction flag, and the system prompt's size. It never holds chat text. `check()` refuses
  any string over 80 characters outside a `words` field.
- Two sources. A shape draws counts from `boltbeam/data/replay_shapes.json` with a seed. It ships `agent-60` (about
  131K tokens) and `chat-300` (about 105K tokens). A trace file is read as it is. A trace can be made from a chat log
  with the standalone converter `tools/trace_from_chat_log.py`. It counts the log with the engine's tokenizer and
  writes only counts and the log's sha256. The system prompt's size is given, because the log does not hold it.
  Nothing in `boltbeam/` imports the converter.
- The replay (`boltbeam/collectors/replay.py`). It starts the user's command with only `--host` and `--port`
  replaced. It plays each turn through `/v1/chat/completions` with the user's template and the prompt cache on. Each
  reply is greedy and exactly the trace's length. Per turn it records the server's `cache_n`, `prompt_n`, `prompt_ms`,
  `predicted_n` and `predicted_ms`, the client's send, first-token and last-token times, and the GPU clock at 10 ms.
- The server's settings come from the user's command, else llama.cpp's defaults, and from its log (context,
  checkpoints, prompt cache). Each setting records its source. The default log level does not print the chunk or the
  cache types.
- Per-turn ceilings (`boltbeam/workflow/replay.py`, `prefill_work.turn_ceiling`). Prefill of the tokens the turn
  needed. Prefill of what the server did (the difference is recompute). Decode at the turn's prefix: the ledger, the
  KV cache and the recurrent state in and out. Peaks are scaled to the turn's SM clock. Bandwidth is not scaled.
- Session efficiency `E` = sum of the warm turns' rooflines over their time. The cold first turn is a row. It is left
  out of the headline.
- Step 5 captures four turns with llama-bench at the turn's depth with the server's flags. A capture counts only when
  llama-bench's measured repetition is within 10% of the server's `prompt_ms`. Otherwise the turn is `unmeasured`.
  One decode capture runs at the deepest turn.
- Output: the text summary, an HTML section, and the TUI. The TUI's Workload page has a replay row, a shape picker,
  and a server picker. The server picker lists the running llama-server commands. A command is used only after the
  user confirms it.
- `replay-diff` compares two replays of one trace turn by turn (`boltbeam.replay_diff.v1`). It is refused when the
  trace, the model or the target differ.

## Runs

- Model: the 27B hybrid, Q4_K_M.
- Server: the user's own command. Context 262,144 tokens, q8_0 K and V cache, one slot, the user's chat template.
- Chip: RTX 5090 with the fixed power policy.
- One run per trace. agent-60 ran with captures. chat-300 ran without them.
- chat-300 read the chip's registry bandwidth (1,693.3 GB/s). agent-60 read the bandwidth measured on this GPU
  (1,693.4 GB/s).

## Findings (where, not why)

- Decode is most of a session: 80% to 85% of the time. It is also most of the loss. On agent-60, decode above its
  ceiling loses 152 s and prefill loses 60 s.
- Every turn recomputes some tokens it had cached: 67 to 795 per turn. The server reuses less than the prompt plus
  the reply it made. Recompute costs 4 s to 6 s per session.
- The time outside `prompt_ms` grows with depth on chat-300. It is 25 ms at turn 2, 233 ms at 56K and 462 ms at
  108K. Short turns at depth spend more of their TTFT there than in the prompt. This settles scope risk 1 as a
  where: that time is outside the server's `prompt_ms`, so the replay counts it as overhead. A trace of the live
  server would be needed to name the part.
- Captured turns (agent-60):
  - Turn 2 (early) is `unmeasured`. llama-bench took 594.8 ms. The server took 704.6 ms.
  - Turns 31, 60 and 56 tie out. The server's prompt path is 111 ms to 180 ms above the bench's.
  - In those turns attention is the largest row. At 128K deep it takes 1,305 ms against a 366 ms ceiling, at 28% MFU.
  - `mul_mat_q` (383 ms to 524 ms) has no ceiling. No role could be paired with its launches.
  - The recurrent scan runs at about 6% MFU against its fp32 ceiling.
- Decode at 130,851 tokens: the kernels take 19.9 ms per token against a 13.65 ms limit. The server's own token
  takes 20.6 ms. The FFN gate and up roles were not paired in that capture, so their time is in the "no role" row.

## Open

- The `mul_mat_q` launches in the deep prefill captures and the FFN gate and up roles in the deep decode capture are
  not paired with roles. Their time is real. Their ceilings are not known.
  - Both are fixed (2026-10-11). Each was confirmed on one new capture of the 27B Q4_K_M on the RTX 5090.
  - Prefill. A turn longer than one ubatch runs in chunks of different widths (795 tokens at `-ub 512` is 512 and
    283). Two things broke the pairing:
    - One role's launches in the narrow chunk run about 1.8 times shorter, past the 1.3 cluster gap, so one
      clustering over both chunks followed chunk widths, not roles.
    - llama.cpp launches another `mul_mat_q` variant for the narrow chunk (tile 96 against 128). Each variant is
      its own template, present in one chunk only, so no template held a role's whole count.
    - Fix: `prefill_attribution.chunk_of` finds each chunk's boundary as the launch where every kernel stem has run
      its share of launches. Each template is cut per chunk width, and each width pairs as a prefill of equal
      chunks. Its role rows say "(512-token chunks)" and "(283-token chunks)". Equal chunks are unchanged.
    - Capture: 795 tokens after 108,000 cached, `-fa 1 -ub 512 -b 2048`. Before: `mul_mat_q` 77.3 ms had no role,
      and attn_qo was ambiguous. After: every `mul_mat_q` launch is paired, nothing is ambiguous, and the int8
      rows run at 26 to 31% MFU in both widths. Attention takes 388.8 ms at 26% MFU.
  - Decode. Two causes:
    - The decode trace read the ceiling of the run's own workload. A replay run's workload is not "decode", so its
      roles carried the prefill block's bytes (26.0 GB a token against 18.3). The fused gate+up kernel then looked
      faster than the memory (2,197 GB/s). Fix: `attribution.provider_trace` always reads the decode ceiling.
    - Deep in a context flash attention runs 16 times a token, like attn_qo, and longer per call than a GEMV. By
      count and bytes alone it fits that role. Fix: decode pairing skips kernels the tie-out names as attention or
      recurrent (`tie_out.kind_of`), as prefill already did.
    - Capture: 63 tokens at 130,000. After: no role unpaired. The GEMVs run at 1,183 to 1,716 GB/s.
- The early turn's capture is `unmeasured`: it differs from the server by 16%.
- The chat-log converter needs the system prompt's size. A log that records each turn's prompt tokens would remove
  that input.
- chat-300's words changed after these runs, so its shape's sha256 changed too. `replay-diff` refuses to compare a
  new chat-300 run with the run above.
- vLLM and Ollama replay adapters are not built. Apple telemetry and peaks are not built.
- The diff has a JSON output and a text output. It has no HTML section and no TUI page yet.

## vLLM against llama.cpp on the same trace (2026-10-11)

One vLLM run replayed the llama.cpp chat-300 run's own trace file (the same counts and sha256).
- vLLM 0.31.0, OpenAI server, the 27B hybrid as AWQ INT4 (g128).
- One sequence, prefix caching on (as llama.cpp's prompt cache), the same chat template, `--max-model-len 114688`.
- A first start with 131,072 tokens was refused by vLLM: 8.33 GiB of KV cache needed, 8.11 GiB free. No turn ran.
  That is the reason for the one repeat.
- vLLM's timings per turn are the deltas of its own `/metrics` counters: prefix-cache hits, prompt and generated
  tokens, prefill and decode seconds. One request runs at a time, so each delta is that turn's.
- vLLM does not log its chunk size. The ceiling takes one chunk per turn, the fewest weight reads. At these turn
  sizes this changes nothing.
- No llama-bench capture exists for vLLM's turns, so the vLLM run has no where tables.
- Both runs read the chip's registry bandwidth (1,693.3 GB/s).

The work matches: 300 of 300 turns had the same reply length and a prefix within 1%. The prefixes differ by at most
259 tokens (0.24%). The engines render the replies they made, and those differ in text.

Where the two differ:
- Decode is the largest part on both. vLLM decodes 21% faster and loses 96 s above its decode ceiling against
  llama.cpp's 178 s. vLLM's ceiling is its own: AWQ weights (15.24 GB per token) and a bf16 KV cache.
- vLLM recomputes more. With prefix caching on this hybrid, vLLM sets its cache block to 784 tokens, so a turn
  reuses only whole blocks and recomputes the rest: up to 849 tokens a turn, 43.8 s in all. llama.cpp's
  checkpoints recompute 5.8 s. At depth this makes vLLM's TTFT the slower one.
- llama.cpp's overhead outside the prompt time grows with depth (above). vLLM's stays near 200 ms: 27.6 s in all.
- vLLM's model load takes 93 s, against 6 s.
- Attention's paths in vLLM come from its package's libraries: bf16 and fp16 with fp32 accumulate, equal sites.
